# Voice and copy

Team1 communication is helpful, respectful, accurate, warm, community-first, and concrete. It should sound like a capable person speaking to another person—not a corporation performing enthusiasm or an agent filling space.

## Writing rules

- Lead with what the audience can understand or do.
- Prefer short sentences, specific nouns, active verbs, and plain language.
- Explain necessary technical terms; remove corporate, crypto, and AI jargon that adds no meaning.
- Use one dominant message and one action. “Less is more” applies to copy as well as layout.
- Verify claims, counts, dates, locations, program status, partner relationships, and destinations. Date mutable facts.
- State Team1's actual role. Do not imply Ava Labs, Avalanche Foundation, sponsor, venue, or partner approval that has not been granted.
- Use original, audience-aware phrasing. Remove generic superlatives, hype, token-promotion language, and fabricated urgency.

## Name and case

`Team1` is allowed at the beginning of a sentence, in a title, or as a proper-name treatment. `team1` is acceptable in flowing copy. Never write `TEAM1`. Keep the approved lowercase `t` inside every emblem; do not type the word to imitate a logo.

## Calls to action

Use a direct verb and name the destination: “Register for the meetup,” “Read the guide,” or “Join the community.” Pair every QR with visible destination/action text. Do not publish an action until its URL, owner, privacy context, availability, and expiry are verified.

## Localization and controlled terms

Write naturally in the audience's language rather than translating word for word. Preserve diacritics and local date/time conventions, and obtain a fluent review for public work. Keep a controlled vocabulary with an owner and effective date for program and role names; when terminology changes, document the deprecated term instead of mixing versions.
