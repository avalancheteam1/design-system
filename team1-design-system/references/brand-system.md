# Team1 brand system

## What is authoritative

The visual authority is the supplied Team1 presentation system, not a newly invented interpretation of “Avalanche branding.” Use authentic assets, baked backgrounds, inherited layouts, and semantic tokens. When this guide says **observed**, it is directly evidenced in the source deck. When it says **derived**, it is a practical recommendation for a new medium and must not be presented as an official brand law.

## Canvas and atmosphere — observed

- Presentation geometry is 16:9, 10 × 5.625 inches. The audited inspection frame is 960 × 540 px.
- The dominant canvas is black with a fine gray technical grid and restrained red atmospheric glows.
- Standard content frames use `assets/backgrounds/team1-grid-framed-dark.png`: red inset perimeter, square corner anchors, and red corner/edge glow.
- Covers and photo mosaics use the quieter `assets/backgrounds/team1-grid-unframed-dark.png`.
- Light framed and unframed variants exist, but the original visible deck used dark layouts throughout. Treat light mode as supported but less established.
- The visible background is a full-slide raster asset. If it is removed, the presentation theme can expose a white fallback. Always inspect the render.
- Standard presentation chrome is a small Team1 mark at lower-left, Avalanche endorsement at lower-right, and a tiny centered page number.

Do not decorate every corner further. The grid and glow are atmosphere; the message remains the first read.

## Color roles — observed

| Role | Value | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Canvas | `#000000` | Primary field |
| Primary type | `#FFFFFF` | Narrative, titles, proof |
| Content signal | `#FF394A` | Metrics, labels, first-read emphasis |
| Structural / Avalanche red | `#E84142` | Dividers, frame logic, Avalanche-origin accents |
| Theme accent | `#F5384B` | Existing theme-linked elements |
| Card surface | `#141414` | Comparisons, cautions, decision points |
| Theme charcoal | `#161618` | Secondary dark surface |
| Near-white | `#F5F5F9` | Softer light surface/type role |
| Digital blue | `#3055B3` | Secondary source accent, used sparingly |
| Bright blue | `#348BFF` | Secondary source accent, used sparingly |

The three reds are close but intentional. Preserve the color already attached to an inherited object. For new content, select the semantic role; do not normalize the deck to one red.

Use red as signal, not wallpaper. White carries most communication. Keep body copy out of the brightest glows and maintain strong contrast.

## Typography — observed

The source contains two real modes:

| Mode | Display / heading | Body | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overview / brand | Kanit Medium | Inter / Inter SemiBold | Introductions, identity, metrics, program overview, community event decks |
| Editorial / case study | Helvetica Neue | Helvetica Neue | Denser narrative, comparisons, case studies, longer modules |

Common source presentation tiers include Kanit Medium 24–32 pt titles, Kanit Medium 13–18 pt red module headings, Inter 13–17 pt body, 32–43 pt red metrics, and a 6 pt Inter SemiBold page marker. Helvetica Neue source layouts commonly use 18–24 pt titles and 12–16 pt narrative copy.

These are presentation observations, not universal pixel sizes. Preserve the inherited object’s type treatment whenever possible. Shorten copy before shrinking type. Do not mix the two modes casually inside one artifact.

Arial is a theme fallback, not a Team1 brand face. The PowerPoint contains subset font data; new characters may still substitute on another machine. Before final delivery, verify installed fonts, glyph coverage, line breaks, and the exported render. No font binaries are bundled with this skill.

## Identity assets — observed

- Primary Team1 wordmark: `assets/logos/team1-wordmark-white-red.png`.
- Compact Team1 mark: `assets/logos/team1-mark-white-red.png`; use for footer-scale or compact placements, not as a substitute for the full wordmark when the host identity must be explicit.
- Avalanche endorsement on dark: `assets/logos/avalanche-wordmark-white.png`.
- Avalanche endorsement on light: `assets/logos/avalanche-wordmark-red.png`.
- Ambassador DAO and regional identities are source-specific. Use only when the relationship and status are verified.

Never redraw, typeset, recolor, stretch, rotate, crop from a screenshot, generate, outline, or add effects to a mark. Preserve aspect ratio and transparent padding. There is no evidenced official numerical clear-space or minimum-size rule in the source. Use enough open space for the mark to remain distinct and request an owner-approved rule when legal precision matters.

Team1 is the primary identity in Team1-hosted work. Avalanche appears as endorsement, not as a merged homemade lockup. Keep partner identities authentic and optically secondary unless an approved co-branding agreement says otherwise.

## Graphic devices — observed

- The technical grid, red glows, corner anchors, and fine perimeter frame are the connective system.
- Gradient chapter tiles combine red with blue, violet, green, or yellow. Use the original raster assets; do not recreate or recolor them.
- Metric icon tiles use simple red/white line art on black and include baked claims. Verify every number before reuse.
- The community-mosaic Team1 and Avalanche marks are expressive hero graphics, not primary logos.
- The cinematic red Team1 hero is suitable for an emotional close or high-energy call to action, not every page.

## Photography — observed

Photography is documentary and community-first: real events, groups, people building, talks in progress, branded physical material, and lived environments. Favor participation over posing. Crops can feel dense and active, but faces and key actions must remain intact. Pair one contextual scene with one detail/brand scene when two images are used.

Avoid generic stock, synthetic crowds, fake UI, coins, chains, neon finance imagery, and broad “Web3” clichés. Do not apply heavy filters that distort skin tones or make every image red. Verify reuse rights and context before external publication.

## Voice and density — derived from established Team1 community work

Write plainly and naturally in the audience’s language. Prefer short, concrete claims and human calls to action. Avoid corporate buzzwords and unexplained crypto jargon. A slide or tile should have one dominant message, one supporting proof layer, and one action at most. Generous negative space is part of the system.
